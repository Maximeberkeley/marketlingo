# biotech: hand-written lessons, digest for Deep Case authors

## Day 1: How a drug is discovered
- OPEN: The average approved drug spent 10.5 years in human trials and review. | A study of 9,704 development programs from 2011 to 2020 found it took an average of 10.5 years for a drug to go from its first human trial to approval. Before that clock starts, scientists spend years in discovery: choosing what to aim at, finding molecules that hit it, and refining one into a candidate safe enough to test in people. This lesson walks through those discovery stages, the part of the pipeline that decides what ever reaches a patient.  [src: https://go.bio.org/rs/490-EHZ-999/images/ClinicalDevelopmentSuccessRates2011_2020.pdf]
- CARD target: Discovery starts with a target: a molecule in the body linked to a disease.  [src: https://www.cambridgemedchemconsulting.com/resources/stages.html]
- CARD hits: Screening tests many molecules against the target. Those that act on it are called hits.  [src: https://www.cambridgemedchemconsulting.com/resources/stages.html]
- CARD lead: Lead optimization turns hits into a candidate Chemists reshape promising compounds for potency, safety and how well the body absorbs them. The best becomes the candidate.  [src: https://www.cambridgemedchemconsulting.com/resources/stages.html]
- CARD ind: An IND opens the door to human trials Before any human trial, the sponsor files an IND with the FDA. The FDA has 30 days to review it.  [src: https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/RL30989.html]
- CHECK buildChain: Put drug discovery in order. => Identify and validate a target > Screen molecules to find hits > Optimize a lead into a candidate > File an IND with the FDA
- CHECK multipleChoice: A molecule shows activity against the target in a screen. What is it called? => A hit
- CARD lens-curiosity: Every pill started as a target Before any chemistry, scientists pick one molecule in the body to aim at. Everything else follows from that choice.  [src: https://www.cambridgemedchemconsulting.com/resources/stages.html]
- CARD rule: Target, hit, lead, candidate: discovery narrows the field before any patient sees a drug.  [src: https://www.cambridgemedchemconsulting.com/resources/stages.html]
- LENS career: Use the stage names Say where a program sits: target validation, hit-to-lead or lead optimization. Biotech teams talk in stages.
- LENS invest: The stage sets the risk A program still in discovery has years of risk ahead. Know the stage before you judge what a biotech is worth.  [src: https://go.bio.org/rs/490-EHZ-999/images/ClinicalDevelopmentSuccessRates2011_2020.pdf]
- LENS build_startup: Find where the time goes Discovery takes years before the first human trial. Anything that shortens a stage saves programs time and money.  [src: https://go.bio.org/rs/490-EHZ-999/images/ClinicalDevelopmentSuccessRates2011_2020.pdf]

## Day 2: Who pays for a decade of R&D

Pfizer agreed to pay $1.25 billion up front, up to $4.8 billion in development, regulatory and commercial milestones, and tiered double-digit royalties on sales if approved. It also bought $100 million of 3SBio shares, and took worldwide rights outside China.  [src: https://www.pfizer.com/news/press-release/press-release-detail/pfizer-enters-exclusive-licensing-agreement-3sbio]

## Day 3: Who holds the power: patents
- OPEN: Keytruda, Merck's cancer drug, sold $31.7 billion in 2025. | Keytruda's sales grew 7% in 2025 to almost $31.7 billion, about 55% of Merck's pharmaceutical sales. Its intravenous form is set to lose exclusivity in 2028. For any drugmaker, the date a top drug loses protection is the most important number in its future.  [src: https://www.zacks.com/stock/news/2878133/mercks-keytruda-drives-its-2025-revenue-surge-whats-ahead]
- CARD patent: A US patent lasts 20 years from the day the application is filed.  [src: https://www.fda.gov/drugs/development-approval-process-drugs/frequently-asked-questions-patents-and-exclusivity https://go.bio.org/rs/490-EHZ-999/images/ClinicalDevelopmentSuccessRates2011_2020.pdf]
- CARD excl: The FDA also grants exclusivity: 5 years for a new chemical entity, 7 for an orphan drug.  [src: https://www.fda.gov/drugs/development-approval-process-drugs/frequently-asked-questions-patents-and-exclusivity]
- CARD cliff: When protection ends, copies arrive Generics don't repeat the original clinical trials, so they cost less. That drop is called a patent cliff.  [src: https://www.fda.gov/drugs/generic-drugs/generic-drug-facts]
- CHECK faceOff: Who has more power when a drugmaker faces a patent cliff? => A biotech with a working drug
- CHECK theCall: What is your move? => License or buy drugs to replace it
- CARD lens-curiosity: Why generics are so cheap Generics skip the original clinical trials. With five competitors, prices can drop nearly 85%.  [src: https://www.fda.gov/drugs/generic-drugs/generic-drug-facts]
- CARD rule: In pharma, the patent clock decides who holds pricing power, and for how long.  [src: https://www.fda.gov/drugs/development-approval-process-drugs/frequently-asked-questions-patents-and-exclusivity https://www.fda.gov/drugs/generic-drugs/generic-drug-facts]
- LENS career: Ask about the patent clock Ask when the company's top drugs lose exclusivity and what will replace them. It shows you understand the business.  [src: https://www.fda.gov/drugs/development-approval-process-drugs/frequently-asked-questions-patents-and-exclusivity]
- LENS invest: Map the patent cliffs A drugmaker's value depends on when its biggest drugs lose protection. Keytruda's 2028 date is central to Merck's outlook.  [src: https://www.zacks.com/stock/news/2878133/mercks-keytruda-drives-its-2025-revenue-surge-whats-ahead]
- LENS build_startup: Patent cliffs create buyers Drugmakers facing a cliff need new drugs fast. Their deadline is what gives smaller biotechs bargaining power.  [src: https://www.zacks.com/stock/news/2878133/mercks-keytruda-drives-its-2025-revenue-surge-whats-ahead https://www.pfizer.com/news/press-release/press-release-detail/pfizer-enters-exclusive-licensing-agreement-3sbio]

## Day 4: The odds every drug faces
- OPEN: Of every 100 drugs that enter human trials, about 8 get approved. | BIO, Informa and QLS Advisors analyzed 12,728 phase transitions across 9,704 development programs from 2011 to 2020. The overall likelihood of approval from Phase 1 was 7.9%, and successful drugs took an average of 10.5 years from Phase 1 to approval. For cancer drugs the likelihood was just 5.3%.  [src: https://go.bio.org/rs/490-EHZ-999/images/ClinicalDevelopmentSuccessRates2011_2020.pdf]
- CARD phases: Each phase has its own odds Phase 1 to 2: 52%, and Phase 2 to 3: 28.9%.  [src: https://go.bio.org/rs/490-EHZ-999/images/ClinicalDevelopmentSuccessRates2011_2020.pdf]
- CARD p2: Phase 2 is the hardest step: only 28.9% of drugs pass it.  [src: https://go.bio.org/rs/490-EHZ-999/images/ClinicalDevelopmentSuccessRates2011_2020.pdf https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/RL30989.html]
- CARD onc: Cancer drugs have it harder: 5.3% reach approval from Phase 1.  [src: https://go.bio.org/rs/490-EHZ-999/images/ClinicalDevelopmentSuccessRates2011_2020.pdf]
- CHECK numberSense: Of 100 drugs entering Phase 1, about how many reach approval? => 8drugs
- CHECK multipleChoice: Which step do the fewest drugs pass? => Phase 2 to Phase 3
- CARD lens-curiosity: Your medicine was a long shot Every approved drug beat odds of about 1 in 13, after 10.5 years of trials and review on average.  [src: https://go.bio.org/rs/490-EHZ-999/images/ClinicalDevelopmentSuccessRates2011_2020.pdf]
- CARD rule: A drug's value is its potential payoff times its odds of getting there.  [src: https://go.bio.org/rs/490-EHZ-999/images/ClinicalDevelopmentSuccessRates2011_2020.pdf]
- LENS career: Quote the 7.9% Knowing that 7.9% of drugs reach approval from Phase 1 frames every pipeline conversation you'll have.  [src: https://go.bio.org/rs/490-EHZ-999/images/ClinicalDevelopmentSuccessRates2011_2020.pdf]
- LENS invest: Risk-adjust every program Multiply potential sales by the odds. A Phase 1 drug has about 7.9%; one that passed Phase 2 has about 52%.  [src: https://go.bio.org/rs/490-EHZ-999/images/ClinicalDevelopmentSuccessRates2011_2020.pdf]
- LENS build_startup: Better odds are worth billions Anything that lifts Phase 2 success, today 28.9%, changes the economics of the whole industry.  [src: https://go.bio.org/rs/490-EHZ-999/images/ClinicalDevelopmentSuccessRates2011_2020.pdf]

## Day 5: Why every drug must prove it works
- OPEN: In the early 1960s, thalidomide caused birth defects in thousands of babies in Western Europe. | Thalidomide was prescribed to pregnant women for morning sickness. Its US application was still pending when the harm became clear, because an FDA reviewer had serious doubts about its safety, so it was never marketed in the United States. The publicity led to congressional hearings and to the 1962 Kefauver-Harris Amendments, which kept the safety requirements and added a new one: proof that the drug works.  [src: https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/RL30989.html]
- CARD law: Drugmakers must prove a drug works, not just that it is safe.  [src: https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/RL30989.html]
- CARD trials: Trials grow phase by phase Phase 1 tests safety in fewer than 100 people. Phase 2 tests whether it works in several hundred.  [src: https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/RL30989.html]
- CARD approvals: The FDA approved 46 novel drugs in 2025.  [src: https://www.fda.gov/drugs/novel-drug-approvals-fda/novel-drug-approvals-2025]
- CHECK spotFake: One of these is false. Tap it. => fake: Phase 1 usually enrolls several thousand patients.
- CHECK theCall: What does approval require? => Adequate, well-controlled studies
- CARD lens-curiosity: A reviewer who said no One FDA reviewer's doubts kept thalidomide off US shelves. The scandal abroad led to the rules every drug follows today.  [src: https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/RL30989.html]
- CARD rule: In drug development, the burden of proof always sits with the drugmaker.  [src: https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/RL30989.html]
- LENS career: Regulatory affairs is a core path Regulatory teams plan the evidence a drug will need from the start. It is one of the central career paths in biotech.  [src: https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/RL30989.html]
- LENS invest: Read the trial design A drug can look good and still fail review if its studies are not well controlled. Read the design, not only the headline.  [src: https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/RL30989.html]
- LENS build_startup: Proof is the moat The cost of proving a drug works keeps many newcomers out. It is also what makes an approved drug so valuable.  [src: https://www.lshtm.ac.uk/newsevents/news/2020/average-cost-developing-new-drug-could-be-15-billion-less-pharmaceutical]

## Day 7: What a drug must show before people
- OPEN: Before a new drug is tested in people, the FDA gets 30 days to say no. | An Investigational New Drug application covers three areas: animal pharmacology and toxicology studies showing whether the drug is reasonably safe for first human testing, manufacturing information on its composition, stability and controls, and clinical protocols with information on the investigators. After submission, the FDA has 30 days to object. If it does not, the IND takes effect and trials can begin. If it finds a problem, it can place a clinical hold.  [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investigational_New_Drug]
- CARD safety: Animal pharmacology and toxicology studies show whether a drug is safe enough to try in people.  [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investigational_New_Drug]
- CARD cmc: Manufacturing details show the company can make consistent batches of the drug.  [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investigational_New_Drug]
- CARD plan: Clinical protocols lay out the trial, and investigator details show who will run it.  [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investigational_New_Drug]
- CARD clock: 30 days to object If the FDA does not object within 30 days, trials can begin. If it finds a problem, it can impose a clinical hold.  [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investigational_New_Drug]
