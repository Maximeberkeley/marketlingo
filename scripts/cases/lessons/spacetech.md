## Day 1: Who builds the space industry
- OPEN: In 2025, SpaceX flew 165 of the world's 320 orbital launches. | Launch attempts worldwide rose about 23.5% in 2025, from 259 the year before to 320. SpaceX's Falcon 9 flew 165 times, and 123 of those launches, about three in four, carried its own Starlink internet satellites. A decade ago the industry was led by government agencies and a few large defense contractors. This lesson maps who builds what today.  [src: https://www.jalopnik.com/2067687/spacex-half-orbital-rocket-launches-2025/]
- CARD primes: Primes are big defense contractors, like Lockheed Martin, that build for governments.  [src: https://www.satellitetoday.com/finance/2026/01/29/lockheed-martin-space-sales-grew-4-in-2025/]
- CARD newspace: New space companies like SpaceX and Rocket Lab sell launches and services at set prices.  [src: https://www.jalopnik.com/2067687/spacex-half-orbital-rocket-launches-2025/ https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/04/12/spacex-launches-more-rockets-than-anyone-else/]
- CARD segments: Space splits into a few big segments Launch gets things to orbit, satellites do the work, and ground systems turn their signals into products.  [src: https://www.spacefoundation.org/2025/07/22/the-space-report-2025-q2/]
- CHECK sortSignal: Prime or new space? => Lockheed Martin -> Prime; Defense contractor for governments -> Prime; SpaceX -> New space; Rocket Lab -> New space; Sells launches at set prices -> New space
- CHECK multipleChoice: What share of the 2024 space economy was commercial? => 78%
- CARD lens-curiosity: Half of all rockets One company, SpaceX, flew more than half of the world's orbital launches in 2025.  [src: https://www.jalopnik.com/2067687/spacex-half-orbital-rocket-launches-2025/]
- CARD rule: In space, access to launch shapes everything downstream.  [src: https://www.jalopnik.com/2067687/spacex-half-orbital-rocket-launches-2025/]
- LENS career: Know which world a company lives in A role at a prime and a role at a new space company look very different. Know which world your target lives in.  [src: https://www.satellitetoday.com/finance/2026/01/29/lockheed-martin-space-sales-grew-4-in-2025/]
- LENS invest: The space economy is mostly commercial 78% of the $613 billion space economy was commercial in 2024. Governments anchor it, but they are not the whole market.  [src: https://www.spacefoundation.org/2025/07/22/the-space-report-2025-q2/]
- LENS build_startup: Launch is the gateway Every space business depends on getting to orbit. Launch price and availability shape what can be built.  [src: https://www.jalopnik.com/2067687/spacex-half-orbital-rocket-launches-2025/]

## Day 2: Who pays for space
- OPEN: A seat on SpaceX's Crew Dragon costs NASA an estimated $60 to $67 million. | The Planetary Society estimates per-seat prices of $60 to $67 million on Crew Dragon and $91 to $99 million on Boeing's Starliner, against about $90 million on Russia's Soyuz and $170 million on the Space Shuttle. Commercial cargo and crew together cost NASA about $7.6 billion to develop, compared with about $50.1 billion for the Shuttle.  [src: https://www.planetary.org/articles/nasas-commercial-crew-is-a-great-deal-for-the-agency]
- CARD gov: Governments spent $132 billion on space in 2024.  [src: https://www.spacefoundation.org/2025/07/22/the-space-report-2025-q2/]
- CARD nasa: Congress passed $24.4 billion for NASA in fiscal 2026.  [src: https://space.com/space-exploration/in-rejection-of-trumps-deep-cuts-congress-passes-usd24-4-billion-budget-for-nasa]
- CARD fixed: NASA now buys rides at fixed prices For commercial crew, NASA committed a fixed sum and companies carried the risk. Contracts stayed within 3% of their original amounts.  [src: https://www.planetary.org/articles/nasas-commercial-crew-is-a-great-deal-for-the-agency]
- CHECK numberSense: A Shuttle seat cost about $170 million, a Crew Dragon seat about $64 million. How many times cheaper is Dragon? => 2.7x
- CHECK multipleChoice: Under NASA's fixed-price commercial crew deals, who carried the risk of cost overruns? => The companies building the vehicles
- CARD lens-curiosity: Space got cheaper A Crew Dragon seat costs less than half the price of a Space Shuttle seat.  [src: https://www.planetary.org/articles/nasas-commercial-crew-is-a-great-deal-for-the-agency]
- CARD rule: In space, buying services instead of hardware moved the risk to companies.  [src: https://www.planetary.org/articles/nasas-commercial-crew-is-a-great-deal-for-the-agency]
- LENS career: Ask what kind of contract it is Fixed-price and cost-based contracts reward different behavior. Ask which one a team works under.  [src: https://www.planetary.org/articles/nasas-commercial-crew-is-a-great-deal-for-the-agency]
- LENS invest: Budgets move contractor revenue Governments spent $132 billion on space in 2024. Budget fights, like NASA's for 2026, shift revenue across contractors.  [src: https://www.spacefoundation.org/2025/07/22/the-space-report-2025-q2/ https://space.com/space-exploration/in-rejection-of-trumps-deep-cuts-congress-passes-usd24-4-billion-budget-for-nasa]
- LENS build_startup: Governments buy services now NASA's commercial programs pay for services at fixed prices. That model opened the door to newer companies.  [src: https://www.planetary.org/articles/nasas-commercial-crew-is-a-great-deal-for-the-agency]

## Day 3: Who holds the power in orbit
- OPEN: In 2025, 123 of SpaceX's 165 launches carried its own Starlink satellites. | About 74.5% of SpaceX's 2025 launches carried Starlink satellites. The company builds the rockets, flies them and runs the satellite internet business on top, while selling the remaining seats on its rockets to governments and other companies.  [src: https://www.jalopnik.com/2067687/spacex-half-orbital-rocket-launches-2025/]
- CARD vertical: SpaceX is vertically integrated: it builds rockets, launches them and runs Starlink on top.  [src: https://www.jalopnik.com/2067687/spacex-half-orbital-rocket-launches-2025/]
- CARD rivals: Arianespace and ULA charge upward of $100 million for launches similar to Falcon 9.  [src: https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/04/12/spacex-launches-more-rockets-than-anyone-else/]
- CARD orbits: Useful orbits are filling up About 40,000 objects are tracked in orbit, and about 11,000 of them are active satellites.  [src: https://www.esa.int/Space_Safety/Space_Debris/ESA_Space_Environment_Report_2025]
- CHECK faceOff: Who has more pricing power in launch today? => SpaceX
- CHECK theCall: How do you protect your launch plans? => Book early and add a second provider
- CARD lens-curiosity: Space is getting crowded About 40,000 objects are tracked in orbit, and debris is growing faster than it falls back to Earth.  [src: https://www.esa.int/Space_Safety/Space_Debris/ESA_Space_Environment_Report_2025]
- CARD rule: In space, power sits with whoever controls the ride to orbit.  [src: https://www.jalopnik.com/2067687/spacex-half-orbital-rocket-launches-2025/]
- LENS career: Ask who launches their satellites Ask a space company who launches its satellites and what happens if that ride slips. It shows you see the real risk.
- LENS invest: Integration is a moat A company that launches its own satellites controls its costs and schedule. Rivals have to rent that capacity.  [src: https://www.jalopnik.com/2067687/spacex-half-orbital-rocket-launches-2025/]
- LENS build_startup: Launch dependence is a pain point Many satellite companies depend on a launch provider that also competes with them. Alternatives have a ready market.  [src: https://www.jalopnik.com/2067687/spacex-half-orbital-rocket-launches-2025/ https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/04/12/spacex-launches-more-rockets-than-anyone-else/]

## Day 4: The numbers that decide space economics
- OPEN: Falcon 9 can carry about 22 tons to low orbit for an advertised $74 million. | SpaceX raised Falcon 9's advertised price 6.1% in 2026, to $74 million, for up to about 22 tons to low Earth orbit. Divide one by the other and you get roughly $3,400 per kilogram. Rocket Lab's Electron costs about $8.4 million but carries less than a ton, so its cost per kilogram is far higher.  [src: https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/04/12/spacex-launches-more-rockets-than-anyone-else/]
- CARD electron: Rocket Lab's Electron costs about $8.4 million but carries less than a ton.  [src: https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/04/12/spacex-launches-more-rockets-than-anyone-else/]

## Day 5: The rules that govern orbit
- OPEN: In 2022, the FCC cut the time a dead satellite may stay in low orbit from 25 years to 5. | The FCC now requires satellites in low Earth orbit to deorbit as soon as practicable and no later than five years after their mission ends. The old 25-year standard had been a recommended practice rather than a requirement. Operators got two years to adjust.  [src: https://www.satellitetoday.com/government-military/2022/09/30/fcc-adopts-5-year-rule-for-deorbiting-satellites/]
- CARD debris: Over 1.2 million pieces of debris larger than 1 cm orbit Earth.  [src: https://www.esa.int/Space_Safety/Space_Debris/ESA_Space_Environment_Report_2025]
- CARD cascade: Collisions create new debris faster than old debris falls back to Earth.  [src: https://www.esa.int/Space_Safety/Space_Debris/ESA_Space_Environment_Report_2025]
- CARD who: Two US agencies decide who flies The FCC licenses satellites and sets the deorbit rule. The FAA licenses all US commercial launches and reentries.  [src: https://www.satellitetoday.com/government-military/2022/09/30/fcc-adopts-5-year-rule-for-deorbiting-satellites/ https://www.faa.gov/space]
- CHECK spotFake: One of these is false. Tap it. => fake: Debris falls back to Earth faster than it is created.
- CHECK theCall: What is your plan? => Design in deorbit capability from the start
- CARD lens-curiosity: From 25 years to 5 Dead satellites in low orbit used to be allowed to linger for 25 years. US rules now say five.  [src: https://www.satellitetoday.com/government-military/2022/09/30/fcc-adopts-5-year-rule-for-deorbiting-satellites/]
- CARD rule: In space, orbits are shared, so every operator must clean up after itself.  [src: https://www.satellitetoday.com/government-military/2022/09/30/fcc-adopts-5-year-rule-for-deorbiting-satellites/ https://www.esa.int/Space_Safety/Space_Debris/ESA_Space_Environment_Report_2025]
- LENS career: Space sustainability is part of the job Debris tracking and compliance with the five-year rule are now part of every satellite program. Know both.  [src: https://www.satellitetoday.com/government-military/2022/09/30/fcc-adopts-5-year-rule-for-deorbiting-satellites/ https://www.esa.int/Space_Safety/Space_Debris/ESA_Space_Environment_Report_2025]
- LENS invest: Debris is a tail risk A cascade of collisions could make busy orbits unusable. ESA says debris keeps growing even without new launches.  [src: https://www.esa.int/Space_Safety/Space_Debris/ESA_Space_Environment_Report_2025]
- LENS build_startup: Rules raise the bar Deorbit requirements add cost to every satellite. Operators that design for them early avoid trouble later.  [src: https://www.satellitetoday.com/government-military/2022/09/30/fcc-adopts-5-year-rule-for-deorbiting-satellites/]
