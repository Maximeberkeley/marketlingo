# logistics: hand-written lessons, digest for Deep Case authors

## Day 1: Who moves the world's goods
- OPEN: US businesses spent $2.58 trillion on logistics in 2024. | The CSCMP State of Logistics report puts 2024 US business logistics costs at $2.58 trillion, up 5.4% from $2.447 trillion, or about 8.8% of nominal GDP. Full truckload spending was $387 billion and private or dedicated fleets $541.4 billion. Behind those numbers sit very different kinds of companies.  [src: https://www.freightwaves.com/news/logistics-gdp-share-rose-in-24-not-likely-to-drop-cscmp-report]
- CARD carriers: Carriers physically move goods: trucking firms, railroads, airlines and shipping lines.  [src: https://trucking.org/news-insights/ata-releases-latest-edition-american-trucking-trends-0]
- CARD 3pl: A 3PL manages logistics for a shipper, well beyond transportation.  [src: https://scm.ncsu.edu/?p=942]
- CARD 4pl: A 4PL integrates the whole supply chain and manages other 3PLs.  [src: https://scm.ncsu.edu/?p=942]
- CHECK sortSignal: Carrier, 3PL or 4PL? => Trucking firm -> Carrier; Railroad -> Carrier; Runs a shipper's warehouses -> 3PL; Plans a shipper's logistics -> 3PL; Manages the other 3PLs -> 4PL; Integrates the whole chain -> 4PL
- CHECK multipleChoice: A firm coordinates several 3PLs across a retailer's whole supply chain. What is it? => A 4PL
- CARD lens-curiosity: Trucks carry most of it More than 72% of US domestic freight by weight moves by truck.  [src: https://trucking.org/news-insights/ata-releases-latest-edition-american-trucking-trends-0]
- CARD rule: In logistics, ask who owns the goods, who moves them, and who manages the movers.  [src: https://scm.ncsu.edu/?p=942]
- LENS career: Name the player type Carrier, 3PL or 4PL: each hires for different skills. Say which kind of company you're talking to.  [src: https://scm.ncsu.edu/?p=942]
- LENS invest: Logistics is a big slice of GDP US logistics cost $2.58 trillion in 2024, about 8.8% of GDP. Small efficiency gains move big numbers.  [src: https://www.freightwaves.com/news/logistics-gdp-share-rose-in-24-not-likely-to-drop-cscmp-report]
- LENS build_startup: Coordination is the hard part 3PLs and 4PLs coordinate many carriers and warehouses. Coordination problems are where software often helps.  [src: https://scm.ncsu.edu/?p=942]

## Day 2: Asset-heavy vs asset-light
- OPEN: C. H. Robinson brought in $16.2 billion of revenue in 2025 while owning very little transportation equipment. | C. H. Robinson describes itself as owning very little transportation equipment and not directly employing the drivers who haul its freight. In 2025 it had about $16.2 billion of revenue, about $2.59 billion of adjusted gross profit (revenue minus purchased transportation and products), and about 75,000 customers.  [src: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1043277/000104327726000009/chrw-20251231.htm]
- CARD light: Brokers like C. H. Robinson match shippers with carriers and keep the difference.  [src: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1043277/000104327726000009/chrw-20251231.htm]
- CARD heavy: J. B. Hunt owns 18,843 tractors and about 125,000 intermodal containers.  [src: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/728535/000143774926005294/jbht20251231_10k.htm]
- CARD trade: Two ways to make money moving freight Owning assets means high fixed costs but control of capacity. Brokering needs little capital but lives on thin margins.  [src: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1043277/000104327726000009/chrw-20251231.htm https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/728535/000143774926005294/jbht20251231_10k.htm]
- CHECK sortSignal: Asset-heavy or asset-light? => J. B. Hunt -> Asset-heavy; Owns tractors and containers -> Asset-heavy; Employs its own drivers -> Asset-heavy; C. H. Robinson -> Asset-light; Owns very little equipment -> Asset-light; Matches shippers and carriers -> Asset-light
- CHECK numberSense: What share of its 2025 revenue did C. H. Robinson keep as adjusted gross profit? => 16%
- CARD lens-curiosity: The freight giant with few trucks One of the biggest US freight companies owns very little transportation equipment.  [src: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1043277/000104327726000009/chrw-20251231.htm]
- CARD rule: In logistics, you either own the capacity or you sell access to it.  [src: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1043277/000104327726000009/chrw-20251231.htm https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/728535/000143774926005294/jbht20251231_10k.htm]
- LENS career: Know the model before the interview Brokers hire for sales and carrier relations; asset carriers also hire for fleets and maintenance. The model shapes the job.  [src: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1043277/000104327726000009/chrw-20251231.htm https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/728535/000143774926005294/jbht20251231_10k.htm]
- LENS invest: Capital changes the cycle Asset-heavy carriers carry fixed costs through downturns. Brokers can flex, but compete on thin margins.  [src: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1043277/000104327726000009/chrw-20251231.htm]
- LENS build_startup: Matching at scale is a software problem C. H. Robinson works with over 450,000 providers while owning little equipment. Matching at that scale is mostly software.  [src: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1043277/000104327726000009/chrw-20251231.htm]

## Day 3: Who holds the power in delivery
- OPEN: In 2024, Amazon delivered 6.3 billion US parcels, more than UPS or FedEx. | Pitney Bowes counted 22.37 billion US parcels in 2024, up 3.4%. USPS delivered 6.9 billion (31%), Amazon Logistics 6.3 billion (28%), UPS 4.7 billion (21%) and FedEx 3.7 billion (17%).  [src: https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/pitney-bowes-parcel-shipping-index-us-carrier-disruption-increasing-and-thats-good]
- CARD shares: US parcel shares in 2024: USPS 31%, Amazon 28%, UPS 21%, FedEx 17%.  [src: https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/pitney-bowes-parcel-shipping-index-us-carrier-disruption-increasing-and-thats-good]
- CARD total: US parcel volume reached 22.37 billion in 2024, up 3.4%.  [src: https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/pitney-bowes-parcel-shipping-index-us-carrier-disruption-increasing-and-thats-good]
- CARD insource: When a shipper becomes a carrier Big shippers can build their own delivery networks. Amazon now delivers more US parcels than UPS or FedEx.  [src: https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/pitney-bowes-parcel-shipping-index-us-carrier-disruption-increasing-and-thats-good]
- CHECK faceOff: Who has more leverage over delivery prices? => A shipper with its own network
- CHECK theCall: What is your best move? => Grow other customers
- CARD lens-curiosity: Amazon passed UPS and FedEx In 2024 Amazon delivered more US parcels than UPS or FedEx. Only the Postal Service delivered more.  [src: https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/pitney-bowes-parcel-shipping-index-us-carrier-disruption-increasing-and-thats-good]
- CARD rule: In delivery, power shifts to whoever controls the network.  [src: https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/pitney-bowes-parcel-shipping-index-us-carrier-disruption-increasing-and-thats-good]
- LENS career: Ask about customer concentration Ask a carrier how much of its volume comes from its top customers. Losing one can reshape the business.
- LENS invest: Customers can become competitors Amazon's 28% share shows how a big customer can become a rival. That risk hangs over every carrier.  [src: https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/pitney-bowes-parcel-shipping-index-us-carrier-disruption-increasing-and-thats-good]
- LENS build_startup: Smaller shippers need options Without their own networks, smaller shippers depend on carriers. Tools that widen their choices meet a real need.  [src: https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/pitney-bowes-parcel-shipping-index-us-carrier-disruption-increasing-and-thats-good]

## Day 4: The numbers that move freight
- OPEN: Trucks hauled about 11.27 billion tons of US freight in 2024. | ATA's American Trucking Trends 2025 reports that trucks moved about 11.27 billion tons in 2024, down from 11.41 billion, or more than 72% of US domestic freight tonnage. Trucking revenue was about $906 billion, down from $1.004 trillion in 2023, and the industry employed 3.58 million professional drivers.  [src: https://trucking.org/news-insights/ata-releases-latest-edition-american-trucking-trends-0]
- CARD drivers: About 3.58 million professional truck drivers keep that freight moving.  [src: https://trucking.org/news-insights/ata-releases-latest-edition-american-trucking-trends-0]
- CARD revenue: Trucking brought in about $906 billion in 2024, down from $1.004 trillion in 2023.  [src: https://trucking.org/news-insights/ata-releases-latest-edition-american-trucking-trends-0]
- CARD costs: Logistics costs rose to $2.58 trillion That was up 5.4% in 2024, about 8.8% of GDP.  [src: https://www.freightwaves.com/news/logistics-gdp-share-rose-in-24-not-likely-to-drop-cscmp-report]
- CHECK numberSense: What share of US domestic freight tonnage did trucks move in 2024? => 72%
- CHECK multipleChoice: About how much revenue did US trucking bring in during 2024? => About $906 billion
- CARD lens-curiosity: 3.58 million drivers About 3.58 million professional truck drivers move most of America's freight.  [src: https://trucking.org/news-insights/ata-releases-latest-edition-american-trucking-trends-0]
- CARD rule: In US freight, trucks carry the load, so trucking sets the pace.  [src: https://trucking.org/news-insights/ata-releases-latest-edition-american-trucking-trends-0]
- LENS career: Quote trucking's share More than 72% of US freight by weight moves by truck. That fact explains why trucking is central to US logistics.  [src: https://trucking.org/news-insights/ata-releases-latest-edition-american-trucking-trends-0]
- LENS invest: Freight cycles hit revenue Trucking revenue fell from $1.004 trillion to about $906 billion between 2023 and 2024. Cycles move earnings hard.  [src: https://trucking.org/news-insights/ata-releases-latest-edition-american-trucking-trends-0]
- LENS build_startup: Rising costs push for savings US logistics costs rose 5.4% in 2024. Rising costs push shippers to look for savings.  [src: https://www.freightwaves.com/news/logistics-gdp-share-rose-in-24-not-likely-to-drop-cscmp-report]

## Day 5: The rule that limits every truck's day
- OPEN: A US truck driver may drive no more than 11 hours after a 10-hour rest. | FMCSA's hours-of-service rules for property-carrying drivers allow up to 11 hours of driving after 10 consecutive hours off duty. Driving must stop by the 14th hour after coming on duty, a 30-minute break is required after 8 cumulative hours of driving, and drivers may not drive after 60 hours on duty in 7 days or 70 in 8 days.  [src: https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/regulations/hours-service/summary-hours-service-regulations]
- CARD window: Driving must end by the 14th hour after coming on duty, even if the driver took breaks.  [src: https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/regulations/hours-service/summary-hours-service-regulations]
- CARD break: After 8 cumulative hours of driving, drivers must take a 30-minute break.  [src: https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/regulations/hours-service/summary-hours-service-regulations]
- CARD weekly: Weekly limits apply too Drivers may not drive after 60 hours on duty in 7 days, or 70 in 8. A 34-hour rest restarts the count.  [src: https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/regulations/hours-service/summary-hours-service-regulations]
- CHECK spotFake: One of these is false. Tap it. => fake: Taking breaks extends the 14-hour window.
- CHECK theCall: What is realistic? => Use two drivers or allow more time
- CARD lens-curiosity: Why trucks stop overnight After 11 hours of driving, a trucker must stop. Ten hours off duty come before the next shift.  [src: https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/regulations/hours-service/summary-hours-service-regulations]
- CARD rule: In trucking, hours rules set the speed limit for the whole supply chain.  [src: https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/regulations/hours-service/summary-hours-service-regulations]
- LENS career: Plan around the rules Dispatch and planning roles schedule around hours-of-service limits every day. Knowing them is basic fluency.  [src: https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/regulations/hours-service/summary-hours-service-regulations]
- LENS invest: Rules cap capacity Each driver can drive at most 11 hours a day. When freight surges, capacity cannot simply stretch.  [src: https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/regulations/hours-service/summary-hours-service-regulations]
- LENS build_startup: Compliance shapes the product Any tool for truck routing or scheduling must respect the 11-hour and 14-hour limits from the start.  [src: https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/regulations/hours-service/summary-hours-service-regulations]
