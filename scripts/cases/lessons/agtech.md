# agtech: hand-written lessons, digest for Deep Case authors

## Day 1: From seed to supermarket
- OPEN: Of every dollar Americans spent on food in 2024, farmers received 11.8 cents. | USDA's Food Dollar Series splits food spending into the farm share and the marketing bill. In 2024 farmers received 11.8 cents of each dollar spent on domestically produced food. The 88.2-cent marketing bill covers processing, transportation, packaging, wholesaling, retailing and food service. After farmers pay for their own inputs, the farm production industry keeps about 5.8 cents.  [src: https://www.fb.org/market-intel/farmers-receive-less-than-6-cents-of-the-food-dollar]
- CARD inputs: It starts with inputs: seeds, fertilizer, chemicals and machinery.  [src: https://ers.usda.gov/topics/farm-economy/farm-sector-income-finances/farm-sector-income-forecast]
- CARD farms: About 1.9 million US farms grow crops and raise animals.  [src: https://www.dtnpf.com/agriculture/web/AG/news/article/2026/02/17/us-agriculture-loses-15-000-farms]
- CARD after: Most of the dollar goes after the farm This marketing bill, 88.2 cents, pays for processing, transport, packaging, retail and restaurants.  [src: https://www.fb.org/market-intel/farmers-receive-less-than-6-cents-of-the-food-dollar]
- CHECK buildChain: Follow food from start to plate. => Input makers sell seed and fertilizer > Farms grow crops and raise animals > Processors turn them into food > Stores and restaurants sell it to you
- CHECK multipleChoice: About how much of each food dollar reached farmers in 2024? => 11.8 cents
- CARD lens-curiosity: Your $10 of food Of $10 spent on food, about $1.18 reaches the farmers who grew it.  [src: https://www.fb.org/market-intel/farmers-receive-less-than-6-cents-of-the-food-dollar]
- CARD rule: In food, most of the value is added after the farm gate.  [src: https://www.fb.org/market-intel/farmers-receive-less-than-6-cents-of-the-food-dollar]
- LENS career: Name the stage Agtech jobs sit at different stages: inputs, farms, processing or retail. Say where a company sits on the chain.
- LENS invest: Most value sits after the farm Only 11.8 cents of each food dollar reaches farmers. Processing, retail and food service capture the rest.  [src: https://www.fb.org/market-intel/farmers-receive-less-than-6-cents-of-the-food-dollar]
- LENS build_startup: Know which stage would pay Farmers work on thin margins. Processors and retailers sit where more of the food dollar flows.  [src: https://www.fb.org/market-intel/farmers-receive-less-than-6-cents-of-the-food-dollar]

## Day 2: How money flows on a farm
- OPEN: US farms are expected to earn $158.4 billion in net income in 2026. | USDA's September 2026 forecast puts net farm income at $158.4 billion, down 2.6% from 2025. Direct government payments are expected to jump 69.8% to $47.4 billion, mostly from Farm Bill commodity payments and higher disaster aid. Without them, farm income would be far lower.  [src: https://ers.usda.gov/topics/farm-economy/farm-sector-income-finances/highlights-from-the-farm-income-forecast https://ers.usda.gov/topics/farm-economy/farm-sector-income-finances/farm-sector-income-forecast]
- CARD receipts: Farms expect about $540 billion in sales in 2026: $253 billion from crops, $287 billion from animals.  [src: https://ers.usda.gov/topics/farm-economy/farm-sector-income-finances/farm-sector-income-forecast]
- CARD expenses: Costs like feed, fertilizer and livestock purchases total $492.8 billion.  [src: https://ers.usda.gov/topics/farm-economy/farm-sector-income-finances/farm-sector-income-forecast]
- CARD gov: Government payments are a big slice Direct payments are forecast to rise 69.8% to $47.4 billion in 2026. That is nearly a third of net farm income.  [src: https://ers.usda.gov/topics/farm-economy/farm-sector-income-finances/farm-sector-income-forecast]
- CHECK sortSignal: Money in or money out? => Crop sales -> Money in; Animal sales -> Money in; Government payments -> Money in; Feed -> Money out; Fertilizer -> Money out; Livestock purchases -> Money out
- CHECK numberSense: About what share of 2026 net farm income comes from government payments? => 30%
- CARD lens-curiosity: Farms run on thin margins Farms take in about $540 billion in sales but spend about $493 billion on production.  [src: https://ers.usda.gov/topics/farm-economy/farm-sector-income-finances/farm-sector-income-forecast]
- CARD rule: On a farm, margins are thin, costs are huge, and policy fills the gap.  [src: https://ers.usda.gov/topics/farm-economy/farm-sector-income-finances/farm-sector-income-forecast]
- LENS career: Know the farm's income statement Sales, input costs and government payments drive every farm decision. Speak to all three and you sound like an insider.  [src: https://ers.usda.gov/topics/farm-economy/farm-sector-income-finances/farm-sector-income-forecast]
- LENS invest: Policy is part of farm income Government payments may reach $47.4 billion in 2026. Farm Bill and aid decisions move the whole sector.  [src: https://ers.usda.gov/topics/farm-economy/farm-sector-income-finances/farm-sector-income-forecast]
- LENS build_startup: Costs are where farmers feel it Production costs are forecast at $492.8 billion in 2026. Anything that cuts feed, fertilizer or livestock costs gets attention.  [src: https://ers.usda.gov/topics/farm-economy/farm-sector-income-finances/farm-sector-income-forecast]

## Day 3: Who holds the power in agriculture
- OPEN: Four meatpackers handled 85% of US steer and heifer slaughter in 2019. | A USDA Economic Research Service report found that the four largest meatpackers accounted for 85% of steer and heifer slaughter in 2019, and 67% of hog slaughter. On the input side, two seed companies held 72% of planted corn acres and 66% of soybean acres in 2018 to 2020. Concentration has increased sharply over four decades in many seed, livestock and food retail markets.  [src: https://ers.usda.gov/webdocs/publications/106795/eib-256_summary.pdf?v=4878.9]
- CARD seed: Two seed companies supplied 72% of planted US corn acres in 2018 to 2020.  [src: https://ers.usda.gov/webdocs/publications/106795/eib-256_summary.pdf?v=4878.9]
- CARD trend: Concentration has risen sharply over four decades in seeds, livestock and food retail.  [src: https://ers.usda.gov/webdocs/publications/106795/eib-256_summary.pdf?v=4878.9]
- CARD squeeze: Farmers sit between two concentrated markets They buy inputs from a few big suppliers and sell to a few big buyers. That limits their bargaining power.  [src: https://ers.usda.gov/webdocs/publications/106795/eib-256_summary.pdf?v=4878.9]
- CHECK faceOff: Who has more say over cattle prices? => A big meatpacker
- CHECK theCall: What strengthens your position? => Join a cooperative or contract early
- CARD lens-curiosity: Four companies, most of the beef Four meatpackers handled 85% of US steer and heifer slaughter in 2019.  [src: https://ers.usda.gov/webdocs/publications/106795/eib-256_summary.pdf?v=4878.9]
- CARD rule: In agriculture, power sits with whoever farmers must buy from or sell to.  [src: https://ers.usda.gov/webdocs/publications/106795/eib-256_summary.pdf?v=4878.9]
- LENS career: Ask where the company sits Ask whether a company buys from farmers, sells to them, or both. It tells you where it holds power.
- LENS invest: Concentration means pricing power Two seed companies held 72% of corn acres. Large shares bring pricing power, and regulators watch closely.  [src: https://ers.usda.gov/webdocs/publications/106795/eib-256_summary.pdf?v=4878.9]
- LENS build_startup: Farmers feel the squeeze Farmers face concentrated suppliers and buyers on both sides. Anything that adds options gives them leverage.  [src: https://ers.usda.gov/webdocs/publications/106795/eib-256_summary.pdf?v=4878.9]

## Day 4: The numbers that run US farming
- OPEN: The US lost 15,000 farms in 2025. | USDA's Farms and Land in Farms 2025 summary counts about 1.9 million farms, down about 8% since 2018. Land in farms fell to about 874 million acres, 25 million fewer than in 2018, while the average farm grew from 444 to 469 acres.  [src: https://www.dtnpf.com/agriculture/web/AG/news/article/2026/02/17/us-agriculture-loses-15-000-farms]
- CARD size: The average farm grew from 444 acres in 2018 to 469 in 2025.  [src: https://www.dtnpf.com/agriculture/web/AG/news/article/2026/02/17/us-agriculture-loses-15-000-farms]
- CARD land: Farms cover about 874 million acres, down 25 million since 2018.  [src: https://www.dtnpf.com/agriculture/web/AG/news/article/2026/02/17/us-agriculture-loses-15-000-farms]
- CARD costs: Input costs keep climbing Fertilizer costs are forecast to jump 15.3% in 2026. Total production costs rise 4.5%.  [src: https://ers.usda.gov/topics/farm-economy/farm-sector-income-finances/farm-sector-income-forecast]
- CHECK numberSense: About how many farms were there in the US in 2025, in millions? => 1.9M
- CHECK multipleChoice: What happened to the average US farm's size from 2018 to 2025? => It grew, from 444 to 469 acres
- CARD lens-curiosity: Bigger farms, fewer farmers Since 2018, the US has lost about 158,200 farms, while the average farm got bigger.  [src: https://www.dtnpf.com/agriculture/web/AG/news/article/2026/02/17/us-agriculture-loses-15-000-farms]
- CARD rule: In US farming, fewer and bigger is the long trend.  [src: https://www.dtnpf.com/agriculture/web/AG/news/article/2026/02/17/us-agriculture-loses-15-000-farms]
- LENS career: Quote the farm count About 1.9 million farms, 469 acres on average, and shrinking. Those numbers frame any agtech conversation.  [src: https://www.dtnpf.com/agriculture/web/AG/news/article/2026/02/17/us-agriculture-loses-15-000-farms]
- LENS invest: Consolidation changes the customer Fewer, bigger farms buy differently than many small ones. Who the customer is keeps changing.  [src: https://www.dtnpf.com/agriculture/web/AG/news/article/2026/02/17/us-agriculture-loses-15-000-farms]
- LENS build_startup: Rising costs raise the stakes Fertilizer costs are forecast to jump 15.3% in 2026. Rising costs make every saving more valuable to farmers.  [src: https://ers.usda.gov/topics/farm-economy/farm-sector-income-finances/farm-sector-income-forecast]

## Day 5: The risk that binds farming: trade
- OPEN: In 2025, China stopped buying US soybeans during tariff talks. | In recent years about half of exported US soybeans went to China. During the 2025 tariff negotiations China stopped buying and turned to South American suppliers. A later deal committed China to buy 12 million metric tons in 2025 and at least 25 million tons a year through 2028, but from September through March China took less than 30% of US soybean exports, about half its previous share.  [src: https://southdakotasearchlight.com/?p=23253]
- CARD switch: China turned to South American suppliers instead.  [src: https://southdakotasearchlight.com/?p=23253]
- CARD deal: A deal had China buy 12 million tons in 2025 and at least 25 million tons a year through 2028.  [src: https://southdakotasearchlight.com/?p=23253]
- CARD pain: Farmers felt the loss Tennessee soybean farmers alone lost nearly $110 million in 2025. Prices still may not cover production costs.  [src: https://southdakotasearchlight.com/?p=23253]
- CHECK spotFake: One of these is false. Tap it. => fake: The halt raised US soybean farmers' profits.
- CHECK theCall: How do you reduce the risk? => Diversify crops and buyers
- CARD lens-curiosity: Why soybean farmers watch China China bought about half of US soybean exports before 2025. When it stopped, US farmers felt it fast.  [src: https://southdakotasearchlight.com/?p=23253]
- CARD rule: In farming, when one buyer dominates, its politics become your risk.  [src: https://southdakotasearchlight.com/?p=23253]
- LENS career: Trade policy is a farm issue Agribusiness roles watch tariffs as closely as weather. Knowing the 2025 soybean story shows you see the link.  [src: https://southdakotasearchlight.com/?p=23253]
- LENS invest: Customer concentration is a risk A market that buys half your exports can vanish in a trade dispute. Check how concentrated a company's buyers are.  [src: https://southdakotasearchlight.com/?p=23253]
- LENS build_startup: Shocks create demand for options Farmers hit by trade shocks look for new crops, buyers and markets. Anything that opens options meets that need.  [src: https://southdakotasearchlight.com/?p=23253]
