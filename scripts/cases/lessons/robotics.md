# robotics: hand-written lessons, digest for Deep Case authors

## Day 1: Three kinds of robots
- OPEN: In 2024, factories around the world installed 542,000 industrial robots. | The International Federation of Robotics counts 542,000 industrial robots installed in 2024 and an operational stock of 4,664,000, up 9%. China installed 295,000, about 54% of the world total, and now runs more than 2 million. Japan installed 44,500, the US 34,200, South Korea 30,600 and Germany 26,982.  [src: https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/global-robot-demand-in-factories-doubles-over-10-years]
- CARD industrial: Industrial robots are factory arms that weld, paint, assemble and move parts.  [src: https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/global-robot-demand-in-factories-doubles-over-10-years]
- CARD service: Service robots work beyond the factory line, from warehouses to operating rooms.  [src: https://investor.intuitivesurgical.com/news-releases/news-release-details/intuitive-announces-preliminary-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-5]
- CARD cobot: Cobots are built to work right next to people, often without safety cages.  [src: https://ifr.org/news/how-robots-work-alongside-humans/]
- CHECK sortSignal: Which type of robot is each? => Welding arm in a factory -> Industrial; Painting robot on a line -> Industrial; Surgical robot -> Service; Warehouse robot -> Service; Arm working beside a person -> Collaborative; Robot with no safety cage -> Collaborative
- CHECK multipleChoice: What sets a cobot apart from a classic industrial robot? => It is built to work safely beside people
- CARD lens-curiosity: Robots in the operating room Da Vinci surgical robots were used in about 3.15 million procedures in 2025.  [src: https://investor.intuitivesurgical.com/news-releases/news-release-details/intuitive-announces-preliminary-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-5]
- CARD rule: A robot's type is defined by where it works and who it works next to.  [src: https://ifr.org/news/how-robots-work-alongside-humans/]
- LENS career: Say which type you mean Industrial, service and collaborative robots call for different skills. Naming the type shows you know the field.
- LENS invest: China drives demand 54% of the industrial robots installed in 2024 went to China. Demand there moves the whole industry.  [src: https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/global-robot-demand-in-factories-doubles-over-10-years]
- LENS build_startup: Each type has its own buyer Factories buy industrial robots, while hospitals and warehouses buy service robots. Know which buyer a product would serve.

## Day 3: Who holds the power in robotics

China installed 295,000 industrial robots in 2024, a record, and runs more than 2 million, the largest stock in the world.  [src: https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/global-robot-demand-in-factories-doubles-over-10-years]

## Day 5: The safety rules robots must follow
- OPEN: On January 25, 1979, a factory robot at a Ford plant in Michigan killed a worker. | Guinness World Records lists Robert Williams as the first person killed by a robot. At Ford's casting plant in Flat Rock, Michigan, a production-line robot of about one ton had slowed down, and Williams climbed into a storage rack to retrieve parts by hand. The robot's arm struck him in the head.  [src: https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/first-human-to-be-killed-by-a-robot]
- CARD cages: Classic industrial robots are kept apart from people by gates, laser scanners and mats.  [src: https://ifr.org/news/how-robots-work-alongside-humans/]
- CARD standards: Two standards set the rules ISO 10218 covers industrial robot safety. ISO/TS 15066 adds specific rules for collaborative robots.  [src: https://www.automate.org/tech-papers/iso-ts-15066-explained]
- CARD limits: Force limits cover 29 body areas, set from a study of when contact starts to hurt.  [src: https://www.automate.org/tech-papers/iso-ts-15066-explained]
- CHECK spotFake: One of these is false. Tap it. => fake: Cobots may hit people at full force.
- CHECK theCall: What must you check first? => That contact forces stay under the limits
- CARD lens-curiosity: Why robots have pain limits Cobot force limits come from a study of when contact starts to hurt, across 29 areas of the body.  [src: https://www.automate.org/tech-papers/iso-ts-15066-explained]
- CARD rule: When robots share space with people, safety limits come before speed.  [src: https://www.automate.org/tech-papers/iso-ts-15066-explained]
- LENS career: Safety engineering is core Every cobot setup needs a risk assessment. Knowing ISO 10218 and ISO/TS 15066 is a real advantage.  [src: https://www.automate.org/tech-papers/iso-ts-15066-explained]
- LENS invest: Safety design is part of the value Cobots can often run without extra safety measures like cages. That design is part of what buyers pay for.  [src: https://ifr.org/news/how-robots-work-alongside-humans/]
- LENS build_startup: Standards are the entry ticket A robot meant to work beside people must meet safety standards. Building to them early avoids costly redesigns.  [src: https://www.automate.org/tech-papers/iso-ts-15066-explained]
