# aerospace: hand-written lessons, digest for Deep Case authors

## Day 1: Who really builds an airliner
- OPEN: Each Boeing 737 fuselage is built in Kansas, then rides a train for about four days to Boeing near Seattle. | In 2005 Boeing sold its Wichita division, which became Spirit AeroSystems, an outside supplier. Spirit went on to build 737 fuselages and major structures for the 767, 777 and 787. In December 2025 Boeing completed its purchase of Spirit's Boeing work, bringing the fuselage line back under its own roof. The trip itself has not changed. Fuselages leave Wichita by rail and arrive in Renton, Washington, where the 737 is assembled. That one journey shows the whole map of this industry: the company whose name the plane carries, and the suppliers that build large parts of it.  [src: https://www.railwayage.com/?p=27569 https://www.spiritaero.com/company/overview/history/ https://boeing.mediaroom.com/2025-12-08-Boeing-Completes-Acquisition-of-Spirit-Aerosystems]
- CARD oem: The OEM is the company whose name the plane carries: Airbus, Boeing, Embraer, COMAC.  [src: https://www.faa.gov/newsroom/how-it-works-aircraft-certification]
- CARD tier1: Tier 1 suppliers deliver whole systems Engines, landing gear, avionics. A Tier 1 builds a complete system and sells it straight to the OEM.  [src: https://blog.brennaninc.com/what-are-the-three-tiers-in-the-aerospace-supply-chain https://www.cfmaeroengines.com/about https://www.rtx.com/collinsaerospace https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safran_Landing_Systems]
- CARD tier23: Tier 2 makes the parts inside those systems. Tier 3 makes smaller pieces for Tier 2.  [src: https://blog.brennaninc.com/what-are-the-three-tiers-in-the-aerospace-supply-chain]
- CHECK multipleChoice: Collins Aerospace sells avionics directly to Boeing and Airbus. Which supplier tier does that make Collins? => Tier 1
- CARD mro: MRO keeps the plane flying after delivery Maintenance, repair and overhaul. Airlines, engine makers and independent shops all do this work.  [src: https://report.lufthansagroup.com/2025/annual-report/en/combined-management-report/business-segments/mro-business-segment/ https://www.aarcorp.com/aar-named-to-top-10-airframe-mros-in-the-world-by-aviation-week/?pg=4]
- CHECK sortSignal: Put each company on the map. => Airbus -> OEM; Embraer -> OEM; CFM International -> Tier 1; Collins Aerospace -> Tier 1; Lufthansa Technik -> MRO; AAR -> MRO
- CHECK multipleChoice: A company builds complete landing gear and sells it to Airbus. Which layer is it? => Tier 1
- CARD lens-curiosity: Look at the engine next time you fly It was built by a different company than the plane. On many Airbus and Boeing jets, that company is CFM.  [src: https://www.cfmaeroengines.com/about]
- CARD rule: The OEM signs for the whole aircraft. The tiers below it build much of what flies.  [src: https://blog.brennaninc.com/what-are-the-three-tiers-in-the-aerospace-supply-chain]
- LENS career: Say which layer a company sits in Call Collins a Tier 1 avionics supplier, not just an aerospace company. It tells the interviewer you know where their work fits.
- LENS invest: Each layer earns in a different way OEMs collect most of the price when a plane is delivered. Engine makers earn much of their profit later, from parts and service.  [src: https://bclplaw.com/en-US/insights/pdp-financing-in-aviation.html https://www.janushenderson.com/hub/article/anatomy-of-a-good-company-safran]
- LENS build_startup: Find the first customer on the map A new part sells to an OEM or a Tier 1. A new repair method sells to MRO shops, and each one buys in its own way.

## Day 2: How an airliner gets paid for
- OPEN: Most airliners are not owned by the airline that flies them. At the end of 2023, 58% were leased. | IATA counts 58% of the global commercial fleet as leased at the end of 2023, up from roughly 10% in the 1970s. The share passed 50% in 2004 and has stayed close to 60% since. A leasing company, or lessor, buys aircraft and rents them to airlines for years at a time. For the airline, leasing means less cash tied up and more freedom to grow or shrink the fleet. For the OEM, a lessor is a customer like any airline: it signs orders and pays for planes.  [src: https://www.iata.org/en/iata-repository/publications/economic-reports/more-aircraft-are-leased-than-owned-by-airlines-globally]
- CARD lessor: A lessor buys planes from the OEM and rents them to airlines for years at a time.  [src: https://www.iata.org/en/iata-repository/publications/economic-reports/more-aircraft-are-leased-than-owned-by-airlines-globally]
- CHECK numberSense: What share of the world's commercial fleet was leased at the end of 2023? => 58%
- CARD pdp: Buyers pay in steps: installments during the build, the balance on delivery.  [src: https://bclplaw.com/en-US/insights/pdp-financing-in-aviation.html]
- CARD engines: Engines are sold near cost CFM sells new engines at a loss or breakeven. It profits from parts and service over the engine's 20 to 25 year life.  [src: https://www.janushenderson.com/hub/article/anatomy-of-a-good-company-safran]
- CHECK buildChain: Put the money in order, from the order to the years after delivery. => An airline or lessor signs an order > Pre-delivery payments come in during the build > The balance is paid on delivery > Parts and service pay for 20 to 25 years
- CHECK multipleChoice: Why would CFM sell a new engine at breakeven? => It earns on parts and service for decades
- CARD lens-curiosity: Your plane may be rented The jet you board may belong to a company you have never heard of. More than half the world's fleet works this way.  [src: https://www.iata.org/en/iata-repository/publications/economic-reports/more-aircraft-are-leased-than-owned-by-airlines-globally]
- CARD rule: In aerospace, the sale is only the start of the money. Parts and service pay for decades.  [src: https://www.janushenderson.com/hub/article/anatomy-of-a-good-company-safran]
- LENS career: Know which money a team protects An aftermarket team guards decades of parts and service profit. Say that, and you sound like someone who knows the business.
- LENS invest: Read the aftermarket, not just new sales New engines can sell near cost while service profit carries the company. Check how much profit comes after delivery.  [src: https://www.janushenderson.com/hub/article/anatomy-of-a-good-company-safran]
- LENS build_startup: Think about the whole life of the product Aerospace buyers pay for decades of upkeep. A product that cuts maintenance cost can matter more than one that cuts the price.

## Day 3: When a supplier can't be replaced
- OPEN: In 2005 Boeing sold its Wichita factory. Twenty years later, it bought it back. | The Wichita factory became Spirit AeroSystems, the outside builder of 737 fuselages and major structures for the 767, 777 and 787. Spirit also built large parts for Airbus, including A350 fuselage sections and A220 wings. By 2024 Spirit was losing money. A Spirit-built fuselage had also reached Renton with improperly installed rivets, which is why Boeing workers opened the door plug that later blew out on Alaska Airlines flight 1282. On December 8, 2025, Boeing completed its purchase of Spirit's Boeing work, and Airbus closed its purchase of the sites building Airbus parts, calling them of critical importance to its aircraft programs. Two rivals paid to own a supplier neither could replace.  [src: https://www.spiritaero.com/company/overview/history/ https://www.spiritaero.com/pages/release/spirit-aerosystems-reports-fourth-quarter-2024-results https://www.airlinegeeks.com/2025/07/10/ntsb-releases-final-report-on-alaska-door-plug-blowout/ https://boeing.mediaroom.com/2025-12-08-Boeing-Completes-Acquisition-of-Spirit-Aerosystems https://www.airbus.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-12-airbus-completes-acquisition-of-spirit-aerosystems-sites]
- CARD sole: Sole source means no backup When only one company can build a part, the OEM cannot simply walk away. A new supplier needs new tooling, tests and approvals.  [src: https://www.spiritaero.com/company/overview/history/ https://www.faa.gov/aircraft/air_cert/production_approvals/prod_cert]
- CARD engines: The 737 MAX has one engine option. The A320neo has two, so its buyers can play the makers against each other.  [src: https://planefyi.com/guides/aircraft-comparison/a320neo-vs-737max/]
- CHECK faceOff: Who has more leverage when negotiating the engine deal? => A320neo buyer
- CHECK theCall: No one else can build that fuselage soon. What do you do? => Buy the supplier and fix it yourself
- CARD lens-curiosity: Two rivals, one rescue Airbus and Boeing compete for almost every big airline order. In December 2025, both paid to take over the same struggling supplier.  [src: https://www.airbus.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-12-airbus-completes-acquisition-of-spirit-aerosystems-sites]
- CARD rule: Leverage belongs to whoever is hardest to replace.  [src: https://boeing.mediaroom.com/2025-12-08-Boeing-Completes-Acquisition-of-Spirit-Aerosystems]
- LENS career: Ask about sole-source parts In an interview, ask which parts are sole source and how the team manages that risk. It is the question a supply chain lead would ask.
- LENS invest: Sole source cuts both ways A sole-source supplier has pricing power. But when it struggles, its customers may have to rescue it, as Boeing and Airbus did with Spirit.  [src: https://boeing.mediaroom.com/2025-12-08-Boeing-Completes-Acquisition-of-Spirit-Aerosystems https://www.airbus.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-12-airbus-completes-acquisition-of-spirit-aerosystems-sites]
- LENS build_startup: Being hard to replace takes time Aerospace customers qualify suppliers slowly and rarely switch. That makes the first contract hard to win and easier to keep.

## Day 4: Three numbers that tell the story
- OPEN: Airbus has 8,754 planes ordered and not yet delivered. At its 2025 pace, that is about 11 years of work. | Airbus delivered 793 commercial aircraft in 2025 and booked 1,000 gross orders, or 889 after cancellations. Its backlog reached a year-end record of 8,754 aircraft. Divide the backlog by a year of deliveries and you get roughly 11 years of production already sold. Three numbers tell you most of what you need to know about a plane maker: how many planes are waiting (backlog), how many it hands over (deliveries), and whether new orders are outpacing deliveries (book-to-bill).  [src: https://www.airbus.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-01-airbus-reports-793-commercial-aircraft-deliveries-in-2025]
- CARD backlog: Backlog is every aircraft ordered but not yet delivered. It measures demand that is already signed.  [src: https://www.airbus.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-01-airbus-reports-793-commercial-aircraft-deliveries-in-2025 https://www.iata.org/en/iata-repository/publications/economic-reports/supply-chain-issues-continue-to-impact-the-industry-in-2025]
- CARD deliveries: Deliveries bring the cash. The balance of the price is paid at handover.  [src: https://www.airbus.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-01-airbus-reports-793-commercial-aircraft-deliveries-in-2025 https://bclplaw.com/en-US/insights/pdp-financing-in-aviation.html]
- CHECK numberSense: Airbus has 8,754 planes on order and delivered 793 in 2025. About how many years of work is that? => 11years
- CARD b2b: Book-to-bill is new orders divided by deliveries Above 1, the backlog grows. Below 1, the factory is eating into it.  [src: https://www.airbus.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-01-airbus-reports-793-commercial-aircraft-deliveries-in-2025]
- CHECK multipleChoice: A plane maker books 500 new orders and delivers 600 planes in a year. What happens to its backlog? => It shrinks by 100
- CARD rate: Rate is planes built per month. The 737 MAX is allowed 42.  [src: https://www.manufacturing.net/aerospace/news/22952957/faa-allows-boeing-to-increase-737-max-production-nearly-two-years-after-door-plug-flew-off-plane]
- CHECK spotFake: One of these is false. Tap it. => fake: Most of a plane's price is paid on the day it is ordered.
- CARD lens-curiosity: Why the planes you fly are getting older With about 17,000 planes on order, airlines wait years for new ones. Delays pushed the average fleet age to a record 14.8 years.  [src: https://www.iata.org/en/iata-repository/publications/economic-reports/supply-chain-issues-continue-to-impact-the-industry-in-2025]
- CARD rule: Read the backlog for demand and the delivery rate for cash.  [src: https://www.airbus.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-01-airbus-reports-793-commercial-aircraft-deliveries-in-2025]
- LENS career: Bring one real number Know your target company's latest deliveries and backlog. One accurate number beats ten adjectives in an interview.
- LENS invest: Track deliveries, not just orders A record backlog only turns into revenue as fast as the factory delivers. Watch the rate as closely as the order book.  [src: https://www.airbus.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-01-airbus-reports-793-commercial-aircraft-deliveries-in-2025]
- LENS build_startup: Size demand from the build rate A supplier's sales follow how many planes get built each month, not airline headlines. The backlog says how long that demand lasts.

## Day 5: Why a regulator sets the pace
- OPEN: On January 5, 2024, a door plug blew out of an Alaska Airlines 737 MAX 9 climbing out of Portland. | The fuselage had reached Boeing's Renton factory from Spirit AeroSystems with improperly installed rivets, so workers removed the door plug to repair them. Boeing's process for tracking removed parts was not followed, and the plug went back without its four bolts. One flight attendant and seven passengers had minor injuries, and the crew returned safely to Portland. The NTSB found that Boeing bore ultimate responsibility, citing inadequate training and oversight of its workers, and faulted the FAA for ineffective compliance enforcement. It also found that even one of the missing bolts would have kept the plug in place.  [src: https://ntsb.gov/investigations/Pages/DCA24MA063.aspx https://www.airlinegeeks.com/2025/07/10/ntsb-releases-final-report-on-alaska-door-plug-blowout/]
- CARD tc: A type certificate approves an aircraft's design, down to every part.  [src: https://www.faa.gov/newsroom/how-it-works-aircraft-certification https://www.skybrary.aero/index.php/Certification_of_Aircraft,_Design_and_Production]
- CARD pc: A production certificate lets a factory build copies of that approved design.  [src: https://www.faa.gov/aircraft/air_cert/production_approvals/prod_cert https://www.skybrary.aero/index.php/Certification_of_Aircraft,_Design_and_Production]
- CHECK multipleChoice: Which approval covers the factory that builds the plane? => The production certificate
- CARD cap: After the blowout, the FAA capped 737 MAX output at 38 a month Its inspectors reviewed Boeing's factory lines before allowing more. The FAA approved 42 a month in October 2025.  [src: https://www.manufacturing.net/aerospace/news/22952957/faa-allows-boeing-to-increase-737-max-production-nearly-two-years-after-door-plug-flew-off-plane]
- CHECK theCall: What gets the cap lifted? => Prove the quality fix works, then ask
- CARD lens-curiosity: Why flying stays so safe Every plane must match an approved design, built by an approved factory. When a step slips, the regulator can slow the whole line.  [src: https://www.faa.gov/newsroom/how-it-works-aircraft-certification]
- CARD rule: The regulator sets the speed limit, and quality decides when it lifts.  [src: https://www.manufacturing.net/aerospace/news/22952957/faa-allows-boeing-to-increase-737-max-production-nearly-two-years-after-door-plug-flew-off-plane]
- LENS career: Know the quality standard by name AS9100 is the aerospace quality standard, built on ISO 9001. Mention it when you apply for manufacturing or quality roles.  [src: https://blog.ansi.org/anab/as9100-certification-aerospace-qms/]
- LENS invest: Quality risk is cash risk The cap limited how many 737 MAX Boeing could deliver, and deliveries bring the cash. One quality failure can hold back revenue for a long time.  [src: https://www.manufacturing.net/aerospace/news/22952957/faa-allows-boeing-to-increase-737-max-production-nearly-two-years-after-door-plug-flew-off-plane]
- LENS build_startup: Approval is part of the product Anything installed on a certified aircraft has to fit an approved design. Plan for the testing and paperwork from the very start.

## Day 6: One flaw, hundreds of grounded jets
- OPEN: In 2023, a flaw in metal powder forced 600 to 700 Pratt & Whitney engines off jets for inspection. | In September 2023, RTX said a contaminant had entered powdered metal during a 2015 production ramp-up at its HMI plant in Clayville, New York. Earlier inspections could not detect it, and it could cause cracking in engine parts. Between 600 and 700 geared turbofan engines had to come off jets for inspection, grounding hundreds of Airbus A320neo jets. RTX expected an average of 350 jets grounded a year through 2026, with as many as 650 idle in the first half of 2024.  [src: https://gazette.com/?p=1137908]
- CARD origin: The flaw entered metal powder at one plant during a 2015 production ramp-up.  [src: https://gazette.com/?p=1137908]
- CARD repair: Each engine repair was first expected to take 60 days. The estimate grew to as long as 300.  [src: https://gazette.com/?p=1137908]
- CARD bill: RTX took a $3 billion charge It also cut its 2025 free cash flow goal from $9 billion to about $7.5 billion.  [src: https://gazette.com/?p=1137908]
- CHECK buildChain: Follow the flaw from the factory to the runway. => A contaminant gets into metal powder > Parts made from it go into engines > The engines fly on A320neo jets > Engines come off for inspection > Jets sit grounded waiting for repairs
- CHECK numberSense: How long could one engine repair take, by the later estimate? => 300days
- CHECK theCall: What protects your schedule best? => Line up spare engines and leases early
- CARD lens-curiosity: A speck from 2015 A contaminant that got into metal powder in 2015 was still grounding jets years later.  [src: https://gazette.com/?p=1137908]
- CARD rule: In aerospace, a flaw deep in the chain grounds planes at the top.  [src: https://gazette.com/?p=1137908]
- LENS career: Have a view on where it breaks A strong interview answer names a weak point, like engine repair capacity, and explains why it grounds planes.  [src: https://gazette.com/?p=1137908]
- LENS invest: Watch the repair timeline RTX expected groundings to last through 2026. Check whether that date holds before trusting any recovery story.  [src: https://gazette.com/?p=1137908]
- LENS build_startup: Test the gap before building The flaw went undetected and repairs took months. Ask shops and airlines which of those two hurts them more.  [src: https://gazette.com/?p=1137908]

## Day 7: The three approvals behind every airliner
- OPEN: Every airliner gets its own airworthiness certificate, issued for that single plane. | An aircraft design first earns a type certificate. A company then needs production approval to build copies of it. Each finished aircraft gets a standard airworthiness certificate tied to its own tail number, usually the last step before it flies in service. During development, special certificates allow test flights.  [src: https://www.evtol.news/news/coming-to-terms-airworthiness-certification]
- CARD three: Certification has three layers: the design, the factory, and each plane.  [src: https://www.evtol.news/news/coming-to-terms-airworthiness-certification]
- CARD standards: Three standards you will hear all the time DO-178C covers flight software. DO-160 sets tests for heat, altitude, vibration and lightning on equipment.  [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DO-178C https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DO-160 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AS9100]
- CHECK sortSignal: Which standard covers it? => Flight control software -> DO-178C; Autopilot code -> DO-178C; Lightning test on a radio -> DO-160; Vibration test on a display -> DO-160; A supplier's quality system -> AS9100; Built on ISO 9001 -> AS9100
- CHECK multipleChoice: Which approval is issued for one specific aircraft? => The airworthiness certificate
- CHECK multipleChoice: Week 1 review: Spirit builds the 737 fuselage. Who designs and certifies the plane? => Boeing, the OEM
- CARD lens-curiosity: Your plane has its own certificate Each airliner gets its own airworthiness certificate after it is checked against the approved design.  [src: https://www.evtol.news/news/coming-to-terms-airworthiness-certification]
- CARD rule: In aerospace, nothing flies until the design, the factory and the plane are each approved.  [src: https://www.evtol.news/news/coming-to-terms-airworthiness-certification]
- LENS career: Name the standard for the role Software roles live by DO-178C, test roles by DO-160, quality roles by AS9100. Use the right one for the job.  [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DO-178C https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DO-160 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AS9100]
- LENS invest: Certified suppliers are sticky Major aerospace companies require AS9100 from their suppliers. Once a supplier is qualified, switching away takes work.  [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AS9100]
- LENS build_startup: The standard shapes the product Cockpit software means DO-178C, and hardware means DO-160 tests. Both set your cost and timeline from the start.  [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DO-178C https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DO-160]

## Day 10: One in a billion
- OPEN: A failure that could bring down an airliner must be rarer than once per billion flight hours. | FAA system safety guidance for large airplanes, AC 25.1309-1, ties probability to severity. Catastrophic failure conditions must be extremely improbable, about one in a billion per flight hour or less. Hazardous ones must be one in 10 million or less, and major ones one in 100,000 or less. The more severe the outcome, the less likely it is allowed to be.  [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AC_25.1309-1]
- CARD ladder: The worse the failure, the rarer it must be Catastrophic: one in a billion flight hours or less. Hazardous: one in 10 million or less.  [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AC_25.1309-1]
- CARD dal: DO-178C gives software a design assurance level from A to E, by the worst failure it could cause.  [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DO-178C]
