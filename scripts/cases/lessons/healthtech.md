# healthtech: hand-written lessons, digest for Deep Case authors

## Day 1: Who's who in US healthcare
- OPEN: The US spent $5.3 trillion on health care in 2024, or $15,474 per person. | CMS reports that national health spending grew 7.2% in 2024 to $5.3 trillion, equal to 18.0% of GDP. Private health insurance paid 31% of it, Medicare 21%, Medicaid 18%, and patients paid 11% out of pocket. Hospital care was the largest category at $1.63 trillion. This lesson maps the players who send and receive that money.  [src: https://www.cms.gov/data-research/statistics-trends-and-reports/national-health-expenditure-data/nhe-fact-sheet]
- CARD payers: Payers fund care: private insurers and government programs like Medicare and Medicaid.  [src: https://www.cms.gov/data-research/statistics-trends-and-reports/national-health-expenditure-data/nhe-fact-sheet]
- CARD providers: Providers deliver care: hospitals, doctors, clinics and pharmacies.  [src: https://www.cms.gov/data-research/statistics-trends-and-reports/national-health-expenditure-data/nhe-fact-sheet https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/covered-entities/index.html]
- CARD pharma: Drugmakers and their middlemen Pharma makes the drugs, and pharmacy benefit managers, or PBMs, run drug plans for payers.  [src: https://www.cms.gov/data-research/statistics-trends-and-reports/national-health-expenditure-data/nhe-fact-sheet https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2024/07/ftc-interim-staff-report-on-prescription-drug-middlemen]
- CHECK sortSignal: Sort each player into its role. => Medicare -> Payer; Private insurer -> Payer; Hospital -> Provider; Pharmacy -> Provider; Drugmaker -> Pharma or PBM; Pharmacy benefit manager -> Pharma or PBM
- CHECK multipleChoice: Which group paid the largest share of US health spending in 2024? => Private health insurance
- CARD lens-curiosity: Almost a fifth of the economy Health care took 18% of the US economy in 2024: $15,474 for every person.  [src: https://www.cms.gov/data-research/statistics-trends-and-reports/national-health-expenditure-data/nhe-fact-sheet]
- CARD rule: In US healthcare, the one who pays is rarely the one who gets the care.  [src: https://www.cms.gov/data-research/statistics-trends-and-reports/national-health-expenditure-data/nhe-fact-sheet]
- LENS career: Name the side a company is on Payer, provider, pharma or PBM: each has its own incentives. Say which side a company sits on before you discuss it.
- LENS invest: Know who holds the purse Private insurers paid 31% of 2024 health spending and Medicare 21%. Their rules shape everyone else's revenue.  [src: https://www.cms.gov/data-research/statistics-trends-and-reports/national-health-expenditure-data/nhe-fact-sheet]
- LENS build_startup: The buyer is rarely the user Patients use care, providers recommend it, and payers often pay. Know which one would buy a product.  [src: https://www.cms.gov/data-research/statistics-trends-and-reports/national-health-expenditure-data/nhe-fact-sheet]

## Day 2: How money moves in US healthcare
- OPEN: Americans spent $5,683 more per person on health care than people in similarly wealthy countries. | KFF compares the US with other large, wealthy countries and finds that Americans spend $5,683 more per person on health care. Prescription drugs are one example: in 2021, US spending per person was $1,635, against an average of $944 in comparable countries.  [src: https://www.kff.org/health-costs/what-drives-health-spending-in-the-u-s-compared-to-other-countries/]
- CARD premium: Most people pay insurance premiums, often through an employer, and the insurer pays the bills.  [src: https://www.census.gov/library/publications/2026/demo/p60-291.html]
- CARD ffs: Under fee-for-service, providers are paid for each service: each visit, test or procedure.  [src: https://www.healthcare.gov/glossary/fee-for-service]
- CARD oop: Patients still pay a share Out-of-pocket spending reached $556.6 billion in 2024, 11% of the total.  [src: https://www.cms.gov/data-research/statistics-trends-and-reports/national-health-expenditure-data/nhe-fact-sheet https://www.census.gov/library/publications/2026/demo/p60-291.html]
- CHECK buildChain: Follow a dollar through the system. => An employer and worker pay a premium > The insurer collects it > A doctor bills for a visit > The insurer pays the claim
- CHECK numberSense: How much more per person did Americans spend on health care than people in similar wealthy countries? => 5683dollars
- CARD lens-curiosity: Who pays when you see a doctor Your premium, often shared with an employer, goes to an insurer, which then pays the doctor's bill.  [src: https://www.census.gov/library/publications/2026/demo/p60-291.html https://www.healthcare.gov/glossary/fee-for-service]
- CARD rule: In US healthcare, paying per service rewards doing more.  [src: https://www.healthcare.gov/glossary/fee-for-service]
- LENS career: Learn how a claim gets paid Billing, coding and claims move trillions of dollars. Knowing how a claim gets paid helps in almost any health role.  [src: https://www.healthcare.gov/glossary/fee-for-service]
- LENS invest: Volume drives provider revenue Under fee-for-service, more visits and procedures mean more revenue. Watch how much care is being used.  [src: https://www.healthcare.gov/glossary/fee-for-service]
- LENS build_startup: Paperwork is a market Every service creates a bill and a claim. Tools that make that flow faster save providers and insurers money.  [src: https://www.healthcare.gov/glossary/fee-for-service]

## Day 3: Who holds the power in healthcare
- OPEN: UnitedHealth Group brought in $447.6 billion in revenue in 2025. | UnitedHealth Group's 2025 revenue grew 12% to $447.6 billion. Its UnitedHealthcare business is an insurer, and its Optum business includes OptumRx, one of the three largest PBMs. The FTC notes that the biggest PBMs are vertically integrated with the nation's largest health insurers and with specialty and retail pharmacies.  [src: https://www.unitedhealthgroup.com/newsroom/2026/2026-01-27-uhg-reports-2025-results-and-issues-2026-outlook.html https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2024/07/ftc-interim-staff-report-on-prescription-drug-middlemen]
- CARD pbm: Three PBMs process nearly 80% of US prescriptions: OptumRx, Express Scripts and Caremark.  [src: https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2024/07/ftc-interim-staff-report-on-prescription-drug-middlemen]
- CARD steer: A PBM owned by an insurer can steer patients to its own pharmacies.  [src: https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2024/07/ftc-interim-staff-report-on-prescription-drug-middlemen]
- CARD conc: A highly concentrated market The top six PBMs handle more than 90% of prescriptions. Independent pharmacies have little bargaining power.  [src: https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2024/07/ftc-interim-staff-report-on-prescription-drug-middlemen]
- CHECK faceOff: Who has more say over which drugs patients can get cheaply? => A large PBM
- CHECK theCall: What is your strongest move? => Join a group that negotiates for many pharmacies
- CARD lens-curiosity: Three companies behind most prescriptions When you fill a prescription, one of three PBMs most likely processed it.  [src: https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2024/07/ftc-interim-staff-report-on-prescription-drug-middlemen]
- CARD rule: In healthcare, whoever sits between patients and payment holds the power.  [src: https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2024/07/ftc-interim-staff-report-on-prescription-drug-middlemen]
- LENS career: Ask who owns whom Ask whether a company's insurer, PBM and clinics sit under one roof. Ownership explains many of its decisions.  [src: https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2024/07/ftc-interim-staff-report-on-prescription-drug-middlemen]
- LENS invest: Integration is the strategy and the risk Owning the insurer, PBM and care delivery drives revenue. It also draws regulators, as the FTC's PBM study shows.  [src: https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2024/07/ftc-interim-staff-report-on-prescription-drug-middlemen https://www.unitedhealthgroup.com/newsroom/2026/2026-01-27-uhg-reports-2025-results-and-issues-2026-outlook.html]
- LENS build_startup: Independent pharmacies are squeezed Small pharmacies face sudden contract changes from big PBMs. Their pain points to unmet needs.  [src: https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2024/07/ftc-interim-staff-report-on-prescription-drug-middlemen]

## Day 4: The numbers that define US healthcare
- OPEN: US health spending grew 7.2% in 2024. | National health spending reached $5.3 trillion in 2024. Private health insurance spending grew 8.8%, Medicare 7.8%, Medicaid 6.6% and out-of-pocket spending 5.9%. Hospital care grew 8.9%, physician and clinical services 8.1% and prescription drugs 7.9%.  [src: https://www.cms.gov/data-research/statistics-trends-and-reports/national-health-expenditure-data/nhe-fact-sheet]
- CARD lines: Where the money goes Hospital care cost $1.63 trillion in 2024, and physician services $1.11 trillion.  [src: https://www.cms.gov/data-research/statistics-trends-and-reports/national-health-expenditure-data/nhe-fact-sheet]
- CARD uninsured: 26.7 million people, 7.9% of the US, had no insurance for all of 2025.  [src: https://www.census.gov/library/publications/2026/demo/p60-291.html]
- CARD growth: Private insurance spending grew 8.8% in 2024, faster than Medicare's 7.8%.  [src: https://www.cms.gov/data-research/statistics-trends-and-reports/national-health-expenditure-data/nhe-fact-sheet]
- CHECK numberSense: What share of people in the US had no insurance for all of 2025? => 7.9%
- CHECK multipleChoice: What was the largest category of US health spending in 2024? => Hospital care
- CARD lens-curiosity: About 1 in 13 without coverage About 1 in 13 people in the US had no health insurance at any point in 2025.  [src: https://www.census.gov/library/publications/2026/demo/p60-291.html]
- CARD rule: In US healthcare, the biggest money flows through hospitals.  [src: https://www.cms.gov/data-research/statistics-trends-and-reports/national-health-expenditure-data/nhe-fact-sheet]
- LENS career: Quote the big three Hospitals $1.63 trillion, physicians $1.11 trillion, drugs $467 billion in 2024. Those numbers frame most health conversations.  [src: https://www.cms.gov/data-research/statistics-trends-and-reports/national-health-expenditure-data/nhe-fact-sheet]
- LENS invest: Private insurance is growing fastest Private insurance spending grew 8.8% in 2024. That pressure shapes what insurers and employers do next.  [src: https://www.cms.gov/data-research/statistics-trends-and-reports/national-health-expenditure-data/nhe-fact-sheet]
- LENS build_startup: Costs keep rising Health spending grew 7.2% in 2024, to 18% of GDP. Rising costs keep payers looking for savings.  [src: https://www.cms.gov/data-research/statistics-trends-and-reports/national-health-expenditure-data/nhe-fact-sheet]

## Day 5: The rules that protect health data
- OPEN: HIPAA, the US health privacy law, does not cover most health apps on your phone. | HIPAA applies to covered entities: health care providers that send health information electronically in standard transactions, health plans such as insurers, Medicare and Medicaid, and health care clearinghouses, plus the business associates that help them. Apps and devices outside that circle fall under the FTC's Health Breach Notification Rule, which the FTC amended in 2024 to stress that it applies to most health apps.  [src: https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/covered-entities/index.html https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-breach-notification-rule-basics-business]
- CARD covered: HIPAA applies to covered entities: providers, health plans and clearinghouses.  [src: https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/covered-entities/index.html]
- CARD apps: A fitness or period-tracking app you download yourself usually is not one of them.  [src: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-breach-notification-rule-basics-business]
- CARD rule2: Breached health apps must tell people After a breach, the app must notify users, the FTC and sometimes the media.  [src: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-breach-notification-rule-basics-business]
- CHECK sortSignal: Which rule covers each one? => Your doctor's clinic -> HIPAA; Medicare -> HIPAA; A health insurer -> HIPAA; A fitness app you downloaded -> FTC rule; A period-tracking app -> FTC rule
- CHECK multipleChoice: A health app you downloaded is breached. Which rule most likely applies? => The FTC's Health Breach Notification Rule
- CARD lens-curiosity: Your app is not your doctor Data you share with your doctor is protected by HIPAA. Data you type into a downloaded app usually is not.  [src: https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/covered-entities/index.html https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-breach-notification-rule-basics-business]
- CARD rule: In health data, the rule that applies depends on who holds the data.  [src: https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/covered-entities/index.html https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-breach-notification-rule-basics-business]
- LENS career: Know which rule applies Health tech roles touch patient data daily. Knowing whether HIPAA or the FTC rule applies is basic fluency.  [src: https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/covered-entities/index.html https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-breach-notification-rule-basics-business]
- LENS invest: Privacy failures are costly A breached health app can face penalties up to $51,744 per violation. Data practices are a real risk factor.  [src: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-breach-notification-rule-basics-business]
- LENS build_startup: The business model sets the rules A tool built for hospitals may fall under HIPAA, while a consumer app usually falls under the FTC. Who you serve decides.  [src: https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/covered-entities/index.html https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-breach-notification-rule-basics-business]
